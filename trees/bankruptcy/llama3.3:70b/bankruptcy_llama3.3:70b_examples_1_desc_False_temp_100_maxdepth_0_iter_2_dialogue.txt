To induce a decision tree classifier based on the given financial features that predict whether a company went bankrupt or not, we first need to understand what each feature represents and how it might relate to bankruptcy.

1. **Working Capital/Total Assets**: This ratio indicates the proportion of working capital (current assets minus current liabilities) to total assets. A higher ratio suggests better liquidity and potentially lower risk of bankruptcy.
2. **Retained Earnings/Total Assets**: Retained earnings represent profits reinvested in the company rather than distributed as dividends. A higher ratio here could indicate a healthier, more stable company with less risk of bankruptcy.
3. **Earnings Before Interest and Tax (EBIT)/Total Assets**: This is a measure of profitability relative to the size of the company's asset base. Higher ratios suggest better operational efficiency and lower bankruptcy risk.
4. **Sales/Total Assets**: This ratio measures asset turnover, indicating how efficiently a company uses its assets to generate sales. A higher ratio could suggest better management and lower risk.
5. **Book Value of Equity/Book Value of Liabilities**: A higher ratio here indicates that the book value of equity exceeds liabilities, suggesting financial health and stability.

Without actual data, we'll conceptually build a decision tree based on these features, prioritizing those most indicative of bankruptcy risk:

**Decision Tree:**

- **Root Node:** Start with the most critical indicator of financial health, which is often considered to be the ability to cover liabilities. Thus, we start with **Book Value of Equity/Book Value of Liabilities**.
  
  |--- Book Value of Equity/Book Value of Liabilities <= 1
  ||--- This indicates that the company's liabilities exceed its equity (in terms of book value), suggesting potential financial distress.
  
  |--- Book Value of Equity/Book Value of Liabilities > 1
  ||--- Proceed to evaluate profitability and efficiency.

- **First Branch (Distressed):**
  ||--- Given the indication of financial distress, we next examine **Working Capital/Total Assets** as a measure of liquidity.
  |||--- Working Capital/Total Assets <= 0.2
  ||||--- class: Bankrupt - Low working capital relative to total assets suggests severe liquidity issues.
  |||--- Working Capital/Total Assets > 0.2
  ||||--- Evaluate further based on **EBIT/Total Assets** to assess operational profitability.
  |||||--- EBIT/Total Assets <= 0
  ||||||--- class: Bankrupt - Negative profitability indicates significant operational issues.
  |||||--- EBIT/Total Assets > 0
  ||||||--- class: Not Bankrupt - Positive earnings suggest some level of operational health.

- **Second Branch (Not Distressed):**
  ||--- With a healthier equity-to-liabilities ratio, we examine **EBIT/Total Assets**.
  |||--- EBIT/Total Assets <= 0.05
  ||||--- Although not distressed in terms of equity vs. liabilities, low profitability could indicate risk.
  |||||--- Evaluate **Sales/Total Assets** to assess efficiency and potential for turnaround.
  ||||||--- Sales/Total Assets <= 0.5
  |||||||--- class: Bankrupt - Low asset turnover suggests inefficient operations and higher risk.
  ||||||--- Sales/Total Assets > 0.5
  |||||||--- class: Not Bankrupt - Efficient use of assets for sales generation indicates lower risk.
  |||--- EBIT/Total Assets > 0.05
  ||||--- class: Not Bankrupt - Healthy profitability suggests low bankruptcy risk.

This decision tree provides a conceptual framework based on the given features, prioritizing financial health indicators and then drilling down into operational efficiency and profitability metrics to predict bankruptcy risk.         nodes[1] = False  # Not applicable in this branch

            nodes[5] = False

    # Convert boolean values in nodes list to integers
    nodes = [int(node) if node is not None else 0 for node in nodes]

    return prediction, nodes
