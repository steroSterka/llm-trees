The provided features are ratios that can give insight into a company's profitability, efficiency, and leverage. 

1. **Earnings Before Interest and Taxes/Total Assets (EBIT/TA)**: This ratio indicates the profitability of a company's assets, showing how efficiently assets are being used to generate earnings.

2. **Net Income/Total Capital**: This measures the return on total capital employed by the company, indicating profitability in relation to both debt and equity financing.

3. **Sales/Total Assets (Asset Turnover)**: Shows how efficiently a company is using its assets to generate sales.

4. **Earnings Before Interest and Taxes/Sales (EBIT Margin)**: Indicates the profitability of sales, before considering taxes and interest payments.

5. **Net Income/Sales**: The net profit margin, which shows the percentage of sales revenue that translates into net income.

6. **Working Capital/Total Assets**: Reflects a company's liquidity position in relation to its total assets, indicating how well current assets cover current liabilities.

7. **Equity/Total Liabilities**: A leverage ratio that indicates the proportion of equity to debt financing, with higher ratios suggesting lower financial risk.

8. **Equity/Total Assets**: This ratio shows what percentage of a company's assets is financed by equity, as opposed to debt.

Given these definitions, we can infer that the most critical factors in determining solvency would involve profitability (ability to generate earnings), efficiency (how well assets are used), and leverage (the balance between equity and debt). Therefore, **EBIT/TA**, **Net Income/Total Capital**, and **Equity/Total Liabilities** could be considered among the most important features for determining solvency because they directly reflect a company's ability to generate profits from its operations and manage its capital structure effectively.
